About

I am a PhD candidate in Comparative Literature at the University of Amsterdam, based at the Amsterdam School for Cultural Analysis (ASCA) and the Amsterdam Institute for Humanities Research (AIHR). My dissertation reads post-impressionist aesthetics in the fiction of Virginia Woolf and Eileen Chang, tracing how colour, vision and the painterly gaze shape modern selfhood and its anxieties.

My work bridges Anglophone and Chinese literary traditions (Woolf, Chang, Lao She, Joseph Conrad) and combines close reading with digital humanities methods, including stylometric text analysis in R and interactive digital narrative. I have presented at the International Virginia Woolf Society and IAWIS/AIERTI conferences, and I enjoy organising workshops where literature becomes something participants can make.

Before Amsterdam I completed an MA in English Studies at the University of Hong Kong (Distinction) and a BA at Beijing Language and Culture University, with an exchange year at Newcastle University.

Research

Doctoral dissertation · University of Amsterdam · 2022 – 2026

Modern Self And Modern Horror: Post-Impressionist Aesthetics in Virginia Woolf and Eileen Chang

Supervisors: prof. dr. Emilie Sitzia (primary), Dr. Nicholas Carr, Dr. Rachel Esner.

Amsterdam School for Cultural Analysis (ASCA) & Amsterdam Institute for Humanities Research (AIHR), Faculty of Humanities.

Modernism & Chinese Modernity

Woolf, Eileen Chang, Lao She and Conrad, read comparatively across Anglophone and Chinese traditions.

Word / Image Studies

Chang's sketches and pictorial writing; colour, visuality and affect as intermedial practice.

Feminist Aesthetics

Female writing, domestic space and the gendered politics of looking in modern fiction.

Digital Humanities

Stylometry in R, Twine, game narratives, and platform algorithms on Chinese web novel sites.
